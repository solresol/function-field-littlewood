# Neither repeated ray can accept at five

Monday 23 November 2026, Australia/Sydney. For the normalized Lai–Sprang
stream over F₅, let (q,k) start at (62,8) or (126,8) and evolve by
(q,k) ↦ (2q+1,2k+1). At every length n ≥ 0 the first square rejects τ.
Even allowing the length and starting ray to depend on the prime, this
family cannot supply a witness at every prime congruent to 5 modulo 8.

## Exact finite proof

Use the [eight-component parity recurrence](2026-11-15-fraction-free-integration.md).
Its state consists of the phase q mod 4 and the relations at raw starts
q+1+d, sizes (k+i,k+j), for d,i,j in {0,1}. The phase determines all
raw phases at the next merge. The successor thus depends only on this
state, and membership of τ depends only on its first square. Compare
the *complete* reduced row bases over F₅, component by component.

| Initial q | First cycle index | First return | Accepting indices before return |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 62 | 103 | 118 | none |
| 126 | 48 | 63 | none |

The verified equalities at the two ends of each cycle give equality after
every further common suffix of repeated (1,1) by induction. Every length
reduces to an index before the first return. This proves the exclusion
including lengths where a child cofactor vector vanishes.

Run the deterministic standard-library checker from the repository root:

    python3 -m search.repeated_ray_cycle

The root-coordinate calculation verifies each starting plane directly
against the original finite root sums (inclusive seed endpoints 80,144).
It then uses the row-space parity solver at the actual indices. A second
calculation starts from integral series coefficients and iterates the
exterior recurrence in residue coordinates. It checks each form is nonzero
before scaling it by a field unit, compares all eight projective forms plus
phase, and rejects by nonzero contraction with τ. Both computations check
every index through the stated return, including index zero. Runtime 4.32 s,
no random seed. The 22 November split-line regression took 0.71 s.
This is a computer-assisted ordinary proof.

## What the minors remember

The motivating question was whether the ordered pair of nonzero homogeneous
child lift lines determines the full-minor ideal one step later. In phase 3
write I(n) for the ideal in F₅ generated by the ten two-by-two minors of the
two five-coordinate cofactor vectors formed *from* state n (for the next
merge). On the 62 ray, I(2) and I(6) are both the unit ideal, whereas I(3)
is the unit ideal and I(7) is zero. Hence no autonomous update of this ideal
alone works even on this one ray. Zero cofactors are allowed in the ideal
test and require the full relation to decide acceptance. Across both complete
orbits, no identical ordered pair of nonzero projective lines gave different
successor ideals. The stronger line-pair transport question remains unresolved.
Exploratory finite samples at other primes give no all-length conclusion here.

Retire repeated (1,1) on these seeds as a selector covering every admissible
prime. The known F₅ witness elsewhere remains available. A next structural
test is whether switching the digit upon reaching a cycle opens a compatible
exit, and which child relation distinguishes such an exit from repetition.
The test should seek a condition meaningful across primes. The r=1 comparison
remains separate. Neither uniform Δ_p=2N nor the binary frontier is resolved.
